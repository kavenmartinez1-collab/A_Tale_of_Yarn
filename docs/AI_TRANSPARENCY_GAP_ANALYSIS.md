# EU AI Act Article 50 — Transparency Gap Analysis

**Subject:** ArtifexWebGame — locally-run LLM NPC dialogue and dungeon generation
**Date of this assessment:** 2026-07-25
**Assessed against:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act), Article 50, as interpreted by
the European Commission's *Guidelines on the implementation of the transparency
obligations for certain AI systems under Article 50 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689*,
**C(2026) 5054 final**, Brussels, 20 July 2026.

> **This document is engineering evidence, not legal advice, and it has not been
> reviewed by a lawyer.** It exists so that a lawyer — or a regulator — can check
> the factual claims against the code rather than take them on trust. Every
> measured number below was produced by `scripts/test-npc-live.mts` against the
> shipped model on real hardware; the commands to reproduce them are at the end.
>

## 2. What the system does

- NPC dialogue is generated by **Qwen3-1.7B (Q4_K_M)** running **entirely on the
  player's own GPU** via this repository's own WebGPU inference engine. No text is
  transmitted anywhere; there is no server component in the shipped game.
- Output is **free-form plain text**, rendered directly into a DOM chat panel.
- The AI Director generates dungeon specifications as JSON; the only free-text
  field a player ever sees from it is a dungeon **name**.
- We are the **provider** of the AI system for Art. 50 purposes. Shipping a
  third-party GGUF does not move the duty upstream (guidelines ¶27), and the
  open-source carve-out in Art. 2(12) expressly does **not** apply to systems
  falling under Article 50 (confirmed at ¶23). There is no SME exemption.

## 3. Article 50(1) — disclosure. Implemented.

The obligation is to ensure people are informed they are interacting with an AI
system, unless that is obvious from context.

**Single-player game NPCs are a listed example of the "obvious from context"
carve-out**, but it is conditional — single-player, no other natural person able
to participate, and obvious to *all* users including vulnerable persons. The
carve-out was narrowed between the draft (8 May 2026) and final guidelines. We
therefore **do not rely on it**, both because the margin is uncomfortable and
because ¶38 states that disclosure in terms, URLs or documentation "may
complement, though not replace, in-context disclosure".

What is implemented, in the game itself:

| Where | What | Code |
|---|---|---|
| First NPC conversation, once per profile | A system message stating the dialogue is AI-generated, runs locally, can be wrong or offensive, and is not a person | `AI_DISCLOSURE_TEXT`, `src/game/ui/npc-chat-panel.ts` |
| Every conversation, always visible | `replies are AI-generated` under the input box | same file, panel hint line |

The first-run notice is deliberately written for a player rather than a lawyer; a
policy paragraph in a speech-bubble UI is dismissed unread, which would defeat
the purpose. **A corresponding disclosure belongs on the store page too** — that
is a publishing action, not a code change, and it is not done yet.

## 4. Article 50(2) — machine-readable marking. Exemption claimed under ¶88.

Art. 50(2) requires providers of AI systems generating synthetic text to ensure
outputs are "marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially
generated or manipulated", by solutions that are "effective, interoperable,
robust and reliable as far as this is **technically feasible**, taking into
account the specificities and limitations of various types of content, the costs
of implementation and the generally acknowledged state of the art."

**It binds this product.** There is no games exemption and no SME exemption.

### The route we rely on — guidelines ¶88, verbatim

> "Real-time content generation that is ephemeral and consumed immediately,
> without being recorded, stored or disseminated further (e.g. in video games,
> virtual reality applications), may also be exempted when marking is not
> technically feasible and the persons exposed to the content are made aware that
> the content is AI-generated or manipulated (e.g. in-experience disclosure,
> session-level notifications)."

These five conditions are **conjunctive**. Each is addressed below.

| # | Condition | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Real-time generation | Met | Generated per turn on the player's GPU in response to their input. Nothing is pre-generated or shipped as content. |
| 2 | Ephemeral, consumed immediately | Met | Rendered to a DOM panel and discarded when the panel closes. No transcript, no export, no share function. |
| 3 | **Not recorded, stored or disseminated further** | Met **as of 2026-07-25** — see below | This was **broken** until this assessment and was fixed for this reason. |
| 4 | Marking not technically feasible | Met | §4.2 |
| 5 | Persons made aware | Met | §3 — the first-run notice is this condition, not only Art. 50(1). |

### 4.1 Condition 3 — a defect found and fixed

The chat panel previously wrote the model's own free-text `action.reason` string
into `localStorage` via `remember() → addFact() → saveMemoryMap()`, where it
persisted across sessions and was re-injected into later prompts as
`persona.memoryFacts`. **That is model-generated text being recorded and stored**,
which breaks condition 3 outright, and arguably converts free-form text into a
stored artifact for which metadata marking *would* be feasible.

Fixed by `memoryFactForAction()` (`src/game/npc/npc-trade.ts`): a closed,
first-party set of nine memory lines selected by action kind. The model's
`reason` is still generated and still used for the on-screen line, then dropped.
**No model-generated prose reaches disk.** Regression-guarded in
`scripts/test-npc.mts`, including a source-level assertion that the panel never
passes `reason` to `remember()`.

Save files store player state (inventory, position, disposition scores) — never
generated text.

### 4.2 Condition 4 — why marking is not technically feasible

The guidelines are explicit (¶81) that feasibility "is not dependent on the
specific resources and capabilities of individual providers". This is therefore
argued as an **objective** claim about the state of the art, not a plea of
limited means.

**a. Metadata marking cannot attach to free-form text.** The Commission's own
*Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content* (Commission / AI
Office, final, 10 June 2026) states that free-form text "cannot transport
metadata". Our output is plain strings rendered into DOM nodes — there is no
container, no file, and no header in which a manifest could live. NIST AI 100-4
makes the same point: text metadata "cannot travel with raw text".

**b. Watermarking does not work at our output length.** Statistical watermarking
needs a token budget our content does not have. Measured on the shipped model
(`scripts/test-npc-live.mts`, n=18 replies as displayed to the player, control
JSON stripped):

```
min 28   p50 64   p95 129–216   max 216 tokens
```

Across runs, 1 of 36 sampled replies reached 216 tokens; the overwhelming
majority sit near 64. The Code of Practice defines "very short text" as **under
200 tokens** and exempts it (Measure 1.1.2). Independent evaluation agrees that
this length is out of reach: *MarkMyWords* (Piet et al., UC Berkeley, IEEE SaTML,
[arXiv:2312.00273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00273)) finds Q&A-length responses
"too short for watermarks to be detected", reporting 50–250 median tokens to
detect at a lenient 2% false-positive rate. For SynthID-Text specifically,
detection reaches only ~0.3 true-positive rate at 1% FPR at 50 tokens.

**c. Strong watermarking is provably impossible.** Zhang et al., ICML 2024
([arXiv:2311.04378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04378)) show strong watermarking
cannot be achieved under natural assumptions, even with a private key.

**d. No implementation exists in this stack.** Verified across the local-inference
ecosystem: llama.cpp, Ollama, vLLM, WebLLM and ONNX Runtime GenAI ship **no** text
watermarking. Only Hugging Face Transformers implements it
(`generation/watermarking.py`). Our engine is a bespoke WebGPU implementation;
adopting SynthID-Text would additionally require training a Bayesian detector on
≥10,000 samples and hosting it publicly for third-party detection.

**e. No harmonised standard exists.** Standardisation Request M/613 covers
high-risk systems only; CEN-CLC/JTC 21 has no marking/watermarking work item. The
Code of Practice concedes (Measure 3.4) that interoperability standards are "yet
to be developed, except for digitally signed metadata", and sets 2 February 2027
for signatories to implement detection interoperability.

**f. C2PA does not rescue this.** Text support is Appendix A.8 of C2PA 2.4, added
December 2025 and flagged provisional. It encodes one Unicode variation selector
per manifest byte — a ~20 KB manifest becomes ~20,000 invisible codepoints on a
~300-character line, and none of it survives rendering to pixels in a speech
bubble.

## 5. Article 50(4) — does not apply, and must not be invoked

Art. 50(4) covers deepfakes and text "published with the purpose of informing the
public on matters of public interest". NPC dialogue is neither.

**The "artistic / creative / fictional work" language belongs to Art. 50(4), not
Art. 50(2).** Guidelines ¶89 states Art. 50(2) has exactly three exceptions —
assistive standard editing, no substantial alteration of input, and law
enforcement. Several published games-industry briefings conflate the two. We do
not, and no compliance claim here rests on the work being creative or fictional.

## 6. What we are careful NOT to claim

Stated explicitly, because over-claiming to an authority is itself penalised
(Art. 99(5)):

- We do **not** claim the dialogue is watermarked. It is not.
- We do **not** claim to be "exempt" under Art. 50(2). ¶88 says such content
  "**may** be exempted". Our position is that we consider the ¶88 conditions met,
  on the basis enumerated in §4, and we accept that this is a judgement.
- We do **not** claim compliance with the Code of Practice on Transparency of
  AI-Generated Content. We are not a signatory; we cite it as evidence of the
  regulator's own view of the state of the art.
- We do **not** claim a creative-works exemption (§5).
- We do **not** claim games are minimal-risk and outside Art. 50. Video Games
  Europe formally requested a games exclusion on 3 June 2026 and it was refused.
- We do **not** argue infeasibility from our size. Feasibility is objective (¶81).
